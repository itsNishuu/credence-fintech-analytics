What is Credence?

Credence helps a bank's credit team keep watch over loans to small businesses. It asks: if business gets tougher, which borrowers will struggle to repay first, and which files should the team open today?

Who uses tools like this
Credit analysts and credit-risk teams at banks and NBFCs
The data
Twelve fictional small businesses, figures in ₹ lakh

The problem it solves

A bank that lends to hundreds of small businesses cannot recheck every loan every week. It needs an early-warning system: a simple, explainable way to see who is comfortable, who is close to the edge, and who would fall over if sales dropped or interest rates rose.

Credence scores every borrower on transparent rules that anyone can read, and lets you “stress” the whole book at once with two dials: a fall in the cash businesses earn and a rise in interest rates.

The story inside the app

Twelve fictional borrowers, one per sector (a spinning mill, an auto components maker, a food processor, a hotel and more), each with a loan, financial figures and documents. The bank's rule, called a covenant, is that each borrower's cash should cover its loan payments at least 1.25 times.

Key words, in everyday language

MSME
Micro, small and medium enterprises: India's term for small businesses.
Loan book (portfolio)
All the loans a lender has made, looked at together.
DSCR (debt service cover)
Cash available ÷ loan payments due. 1.0× means just enough; below 1.0× means not enough.
Covenant
A condition in the loan agreement, here a minimum cover of 1.25×.
Leverage (debt to net worth)
How much the business has borrowed compared with the owners' own money.
Current ratio
Short-term assets ÷ short-term bills. Below 1.0 can signal cash strain.
Interest cover
Operating profit ÷ interest. How easily interest is paid.
Stress test
Re-running the numbers under bad conditions to see what breaks.
Watchlist
Borrowers flagged for closer monitoring.
Grade migration
How many borrowers would move to a worse (or better) grade under stress.
Concentration (HHI)
How much lending is bunched in a few sectors. Higher means more eggs in one basket.
Expected loss
Chance of default × share lost if it happens × amount lent. Illustrative here, not a real credit model.

What this project shows, and what it does not

What it shows

Turning credit-review practice into a decision-support tool that is transparent, tested and readable by a credit committee. Relevant to credit analyst, credit risk, lending operations and portfolio monitoring roles.

What it is not

It is not a real credit model, uses no real bank or bureau data, and makes no lending decisions. The scorecard is a teaching tool.
